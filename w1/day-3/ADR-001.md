# ADR-001: Build (Self-hosted Open Source Observability) vs Buy (Datadog SaaS)

## Status
Accepted

## Context
As our Payment Service scales, the volume of metrics and transaction logs grows exponentially. We must decide whether to continue using a SaaS observability vendor (**Datadog**) or migrate to a self-hosted Open Source telemetry pipeline (**VictoriaMetrics + Elasticsearch + Apache Kafka**). 

Our current and projected telemetry load across three operational scale tiers is defined below:
1. **Small**: 10 services, 50 GB logs/day, 1M active metric series.
2. **Medium**: 100 services, 500 GB logs/day, 10M active metric series.
3. **Large**: 1,000 services, 5 TB logs/day, 100M active metric series.

Datadog's pricing is $2.50 per GB of logs (ingestion + 30-day index retention) and $0.05 per active custom metric series per month. For a self-hosted stack on AWS, we must account for EC2 instances, GP3 block storage, S3 backups, cross-AZ data transfer, and dedicated DevOps engineering resources ($150,000/year fully loaded salary per FTE).

## Decision
We choose to **Build** (self-host the observability stack using VictoriaMetrics for metrics, Elasticsearch/OpenSearch for logs, and Apache Kafka as the transport layer) starting from the **Medium** tier. For the **Small** tier, we will continue to **Buy** (use Datadog SaaS) to allow our engineering team to focus entirely on core product development.

### Quantified Cost Trade-Offs (Monthly)
The financial analysis demonstrates that while Datadog is convenient at a small scale, its cost becomes astronomical at medium and large scales compared to self-hosted AWS infrastructure (which includes the cost of dedicated engineering overhead):

* **Small Tier (10 services):**
  * **Datadog Cost:** $53,750.00 / month.
  * **Self-Hosted Cost:** $3,300.00 / month (Infra: $800.00 + 0.2 FTE: $2,500.00).
  * *Analysis:* At this early stage, the $50k list price of Datadog can be negotiated down, and saving the team from operational distraction outweighs the build savings. We choose **Datadog** for the Small tier.
* **Medium Tier (100 services):**
  * **Datadog Cost:** $430,000.00 / month (with 20% enterprise discount).
  * **Self-Hosted Cost:** $11,274.00 / month (Infra: $5,024.00 + 0.5 FTE: $6,250.00).
  * *Analysis:* Migrating to self-hosted saves **$418,726.00 per month** (97.3% cost reduction). The operational overhead of 0.5 FTE is highly justified by these savings.
* **Large Tier (1,000 services):**
  * **Datadog Cost:** $3,225,000.00 / month (with 40% volume discount).
  * **Self-Hosted Cost:** $56,090.00 / month (Infra: $37,340.00 + 1.5 FTE: $18,750.00).
  * *Analysis:* Saving over **$3.16 million per month** allows us to fund a highly skilled Platform Engineering team while maintaining data sovereignty and sub-millisecond query latency.

## Consequences
* **DevOps Resource Allocation:** We must hire/dedicate 0.5 to 1.5 FTE Platform Engineers to maintain, patch, and monitor the availability of Kafka, VictoriaMetrics, and Elasticsearch clusters.
* **Data Sovereignty:** Financial logs and PII (user transactional data) will remain entirely within our AWS VPC boundaries, significantly simplifying PCI-DSS and GDPR compliance.
* **Alerting Latency:** By implementing Apache Flink on the Kafka metrics stream, we reduce anomaly alert latency from Datadog's typical 1-2 minutes down to sub-10 seconds.
* **Operations Overhead:** The team takes on the responsibility of managing backup schedules, disk sizing, and cluster scaling.

## Alternatives
* **Grafana Cloud:** Offered a middle-ground pricing model, but at 10M+ metrics/sec, custom metrics costs still exceeded self-hosted infrastructure by 400%.
* **Direct Push (No Kafka Broker):** Pushing metrics directly to VictoriaMetrics was rejected because temporary downstream outages would lead to irreversible data loss. Kafka provides essential buffering.
